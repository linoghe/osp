10 AI Readiness

AI Readiness that Supports Customers

A checklist and RACI for making your digital presence work for AI-powered customers — with skillset requirements and a guide to what Jam and OSP can own versus what to outsource.

10.01 The Philosophy

What "AI Readiness" actually means

AI engines — ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini — are now a discovery and decision-support layer for customers. When someone asks “what’s the best boutique hotel for a quiet romantic weekend in Lisbon?” or “who does the best structured data implementation for multi-location businesses?”, an AI answers. Your website does not.

Make your content work for the customers who find you through AI. Structure it so that when an AI reads your site, it can represent you accurately, completely, and helpfully — because you built it that way on purpose. That requires three things:

  • Machine-readable content. Content that is structured, labeled, and written in plain language — not locked inside JavaScript, PDFs, or visual design that a crawler cannot parse.
  • Semantic markup. Schema.org structured data that tells crawlers what your content means, not just what it says.
  • An AI-native content layer. A parallel plain-text representation of your core content (the Markdown mirror) that any LLM can consume directly, without needing to render a browser or guess at your intent.
The strategy scales, not the scope. The same five pillars apply to a 200-location auto parts group and a 12-room boutique hotel. The difference is the volume of content, the complexity of the schema types, and the number of people maintaining it — not the underlying approach.
10.02 The Five Pillars

What the program covers

Pillar 1
Knowledge Base
Structured documentation of who you are, what you offer, how you work, and what customers need to know.
Pillar 2
Schema.org
Semantic markup that tells crawlers and LLMs what your content means — organization, products, FAQs, locations, reviews.
Pillar 3
AI Site Mirror
A plain-text/Markdown parallel of your key content, hosted publicly, consumable by any LLM without rendering a browser.
Pillar 4
Testing & Validation
Schema validation, AI query testing, and hallucination monitoring to confirm the strategy is working.
Pillar 5
Governance
The processes that keep your AI readiness current as your content, products, and team evolve.

  • Core narrative copywriting
  • Schema strategy (after OSP spec)
  • robots.txt AI directives

The pattern: Jam can own everything that requires business knowledge and AI literacy (what your brand says, what customers ask, whether AI is representing you accurately). OSP owns strategy and content quality. Karim owns anything that requires touching code, servers, or the CMS database layer.
10.05 Scale Note

How this changes by client size

The five pillars and the RACI structure above apply universally. What changes is scope and resourcing:

  • Small / boutique (e.g., a 12-room hotel). One person (Jam) can manage most of the “own now” column directly. Content volume is low. Schema types are well-defined (LodgingBusiness, Room, Offer). Mirror is 30–50 files. Technical work is a small sprint for Karim.
  • Mid-size (e.g., a regional services firm or growing startup). A dedicated content coordinator takes on the content ops layer. Schema becomes more complex (multiple service types, locations, FAQs per service line). Mirror grows to 100–300 files. Quarterly audits become part of a content calendar.
  • Enterprise / multi-location (e.g., an auto parts group). A content ops team is needed. Schema implementation is a dev-sprint per vertical. Mirror requires an automated pipeline. The “Jam” role becomes a VP of Content or Marketing Ops with a team reporting to them. OSP stays at the strategy and QA layer. Karim’s role becomes an internal engineering team or managed service.
The pricing implication: OSP’s engagement scope grows with content volume and page-type complexity, not with client revenue or company size. A small client with a deeply complex product catalog needs more schema work than a large client with simple, homogeneous services.
