Bibliography

Sources consulted across pages 09 (Why a Site Mirror), 10 (AI Readiness), and 11 (Knowledge Hubs). Includes direct citations, background research, and supporting data.

Content Strategy, Editorial & Information Architecture
  • Book Halvorson, K. & Rach, M. Content Strategy for the Web, 2nd ed. New Riders.
    The foundational practitioner reference. Defines content strategy as the practice of planning for the creation, delivery, and governance of useful, usable content. Identifies editorial strategy, content strategy, and IA as distinct disciplines — and the practitioner who spans them as “a rare breed.” Original essay: A List Apart, 2009.
    2012
  • Book Rosenfeld, L. & Morville, P. Information Architecture for the World Wide Web. O’Reilly.
    Established IA as a formal discipline. Rosenfeld later described the content strategy relationship: “If IA is the spatial side of information, content strategy is the temporal side of the same coin.” Cited in UX Magazine.
    1998
  • Article Arango, J. “What’s the Difference Between Information Architecture and Content Strategy?” jarango.com.
    Clear modern demarcation: content strategy focuses on the work; IA focuses on the frame. “Content strategy is to copywriting as information architecture is to design.” Directly addresses why both are necessary and why they are not interchangeable.
    2024
  • Article Information Architecture Authority. “Information Architecture vs. Content Strategy.”
    Comprehensive breakdown of scope boundaries: IA deliverables are structural (sitemaps, taxonomies, metadata frameworks); content strategy deliverables are editorial and operational (content audits, governance models, voice guides). “Conflating the two produces organizational gaps.”
    2025
  • Article UX Magazine. “Content Strategy and UX: A Modern Love Story.”
    Practical observation: “an IA is rarely responsible for editorial, workflow, or governance components of content planning and development” — the exact areas that, when overlooked, blow up project timelines and compromise content quality.
    n.d.
  • Article Kelly, W. “Why So Many Technical and Marketing Content Initiatives Fail.” Substack.
    Frames the discipline separation as a structural design problem, not a personnel one. References Nielsen Norman Group: without explicit ownership spanning strategy, editorial, and architecture, content defaults to whoever absorbs the work — “a temporary patch on a permanent governance gap.”
    2024
Organizational Structure & Content Governance
  • Report Pope, L. “Unlocking Content Potential: A Report on Organising Structures and Capability.” La Pope Ltd.
    Survey of 72 content teams. Finds siloed work and lack of collaboration among the most common structural failures. 1 in 5 content teams are fully decentralised — a “disappointing signal” associated with poor content quality and brand inconsistency.
    2025
  • Data Forrester Research (via Essey Marketing). “Organizational Silos in Marketing.”
    77% of marketing leaders say organizational silos make aligning on a content strategy difficult. Only 1% describe their content planning and execution as seamless.
    2023
  • Report Content Science. “What Is the State of Content Operations in 2025?”
    Research across 100+ content team members at organizations including Amazon, Fidelity, Mayo Clinic, and Thomson Reuters. Finds persistent challenges around lack of integrated strategy and poor cross-team communication. Full 95-page report available.
    2025
  • Report AirOps. “The State of Content Teams in 2025.”
    51% of content teams struggle with upskilling gaps; 82% cite quality control as their biggest challenge. Technical fluency (IA, content systems, AI integration) is now as important as editorial or domain expertise. New roles — content engineers, AI strategists, prompt specialists — are emerging to bridge the gap.
    2025
  • Report WG Content / Researchscape. “The 2024 State of Content Planning.”
    ~170 respondents. Top challenge: getting subject matter experts involved in content creation (39%). 85% outsource at least some content writing. Finds persistent gaps between strategy and execution, and between internal knowledge holders and content producers.
    2024
  • Article Aprimo. “Ultimate Guide to Content Governance Strategy.”
    63% of enterprise marketers struggle to attribute ROI to content efforts due to governance gaps. Organizations using AI for content report 68% ROI increase but only 43% have established responsible AI governance councils. Siloed content and teams identified as the most common governance barrier.
    2025
  • Article 23HubLab. “Lack of Central Topic Ownership in AI Workflows.”
    AI removes natural production constraints, making the absence of a central topic owner catastrophic. Without a governance layer defining topic ownership and content hierarchy, AI pipelines become “factories without foremen.” Directly relevant to knowledge hub governance requirements.
    2025
AI Site Mirrors, Structured Data & LLM Discoverability
  • Spec Howard, J. The /llms.txt file. llmstxt.org.
    Original proposal for an LLM-friendly site index file: a markdown file at the site root that gives AI tools a structured, low-noise map of important content. Elegant in concept; adoption remains unconfirmed by major AI providers as of Q1 2026.
    2024
  • Data SE Ranking. 300,000-domain study on llms.txt.
    Found no statistically significant correlation between having an llms.txt file and AI citation frequency. The strongest predictors of AI citation remain content depth, schema markup, and structured plain-text availability.
    2026
  • Data Rankeo. “How to Optimize Schema Markup for AI Engines.”
    60% of AI Overview citations come from pages with schema markup, vs. 38% for unstructured HTML alone. Pages with comprehensive schema receive 28% more AI citations than pages without it.
    2026
  • Article Derivatex Agency. “LLMs.txt Guide: What It Does and Doesn’t Do.”
    Comprehensive analysis of adoption, limitations, and real-world impact of the llms.txt specification. Concludes that structured content and schema remain the more reliable path to AI discoverability.
    2026
  • Article Zenn / c12o. “Why llms.txt Won’t Boost Your AI Search Ranking.”
    AEO priorities beyond llms.txt. Argues that AI Engine Optimization requires a complete content architecture approach, not a single file. Consistent with the three-layer model (schema + mirror + governance).
    2026
  • Reference Schema.org. DataFeed specification.
    Structured feed specification for machine-readable entity catalogs. Used for hotel room inventory, product listings, and other regularly updated structured content.
    n.d.
  • Article Google Search Central. “Creating Helpful, Reliable, People-First Content.”
    Google’s official guidance defining “search engine-first content” as a failure mode. Directly supports the argument that AI-readable content and customer-first content are the same thing.
    2025
  • Article Bing Webmaster Guidelines. Webmaster Guidelines.
    Content must “fully satisfy user intent” for Copilot grounding. Establishes that Microsoft’s AI systems use the same quality signals as its search index.
    2026
